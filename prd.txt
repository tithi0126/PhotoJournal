Photojournal: PRD & Technical Implementation Guide
Working title: "Frame" · Version 1.0 · Prepared by Product & Architecture
1. Executive Summary
Frame is a minimalist photojournal built on one rule: one photo per day. No feeds, no filters, no likes, no infinite scroll. Each day gets a single photograph, a short title and a few lines of text. Everything technical about the shot (camera, lens, ISO, aperture, shutter speed, GPS) is read automatically from the photo's EXIF data, so the user never types metadata.
Over time the journal becomes a quiet, beautiful archive:
• A calendar to move through days.
• An automatically generated monthly contact sheet and yearly collage, where every tile deep-links back to its day.
• A postcard maker that turns 12–15 favorite photos into print-ready PDFs with correct bleed, margins and resolution.
Pitch: "A year in 365 frames. One photo a day, a lifetime of memory, and twelve postcards you can actually hold."
Why it wins: Scarcity creates meaning. Photo apps optimize for volume; Frame optimizes for attention. The constraint is the product, and the print output is the emotional and commercial payoff.

2. Target Audience & User Personas
Primary audience: People aged 22–45 who like photography or journaling, feel overwhelmed by their camera roll, and want a lightweight daily ritual. They are mobile-first, privacy-conscious and attracted to analog aesthetics (film cameras, zines, Polaroids).

3. Detailed Feature Breakdown
3.1 Core Philosophy: One Photo Per Day
Rule: Each calendar date holds at most one entry, and each entry holds exactly one photo.
Functional requirements
• FR-1.1: The database enforces uniqueness on entry_date (one row per date).
• FR-1.2: "Today" is the default target date. The user may also add photos to past dates (backfill) subject to the rules below.
• FR-1.3: Future dates are locked; the calendar shows them as inactive.
Edge cases
Scenario
Behavior
User tries to add a second photo to a day that already has one
Show a friendly sheet: "Today already has a photo. Replace it, or keep it?" Replace keeps the old image in a 30-day trash (recoverable) and preserves title/description unless the user chooses to clear them. No second photo is ever stored for that date.
User missed a day
The calendar shows an empty, unobtrusive dot. No red marks, no streak shaming. The day remains fillable (see backfill).
Backfill of past days
Allowed. Default: the app suggests the photo's EXIF capture date. Photos with no EXIF date prompt the user to confirm the date.
Selected photo's capture date differs from the target date
Show a one-line notice: "This photo was taken on 12 Mar. Use it for 14 Mar?" with "Use 12 Mar instead" as the secondary action. The user decides; the true capture date is always stored separately from the journal date.
Photo lacks EXIF (screenshots, edited/exported images, stripped files)
The entry saves normally; the metadata panel shows only what exists and hides empty fields rather than showing blanks.
Time zones & travel
entry_date is a plain local calendar date (YYYY-MM-DD) chosen by the user and never shifted by time zone. Capture time is stored as a UTC timestamp plus the original offset when available.
User wants to delete an entry
Soft delete with undo; hard delete after 30 days. The date becomes empty and fillable again.
Multiple devices
One entry per date is enforced after sync; if two devices created different entries for the same date, the later updated_at wins and the other photo is moved to trash with a notification (see conflict handling in §5.4).
Day-boundary at midnight
A grace period until 03:00 local time: the app still offers yesterday as an easy-to-select option for late-night shooters.
3.2 Daily View & Auto-Metadata
Layout (top to bottom)
1. Full-bleed or near-full-width photo, preserving aspect ratio (tap to view full-screen, pinch to zoom).
2. Date label (e.g., Friday, 23 October 2026).
3. Title: single line, max 60 characters, optional.
4. Description: max 280 characters, optional.
5. Collapsible metadata drawer, closed by default, showing:
    ◦ Camera make/model
    ◦ Lens model
    ◦ ISO, aperture (f/), shutter speed, focal length
    ◦ Capture date/time
    ◦ GPS coordinates and a reverse-geocoded place name
    ◦ A small static map thumbnail (opt-in)
Functional requirements
• FR-2.1: All metadata fields are extracted automatically at import and are read-only. The user cannot type EXIF values.
• FR-2.2: Users can toggle "Hide location" per entry. This hides it in the UI and from any exports/shares, while still storing it locally.
• FR-2.3: Swipe left/right navigates to the previous/next day that has an entry; a header button jumps to the calendar.
• FR-2.4: Empty days show an elegant empty state with a single "Add photo" action.
Privacy: GPS is sensitive. Default is location stored, displayed locally, and stripped from any shared image or print file. Postcards never print coordinates unless the user opts in to a caption.
3.3 Calendar Navigation
• FR-3.1: Month grid where each day with an entry shows a square thumbnail; empty days show a faint numeral.
• FR-3.2: Header with month/year and a quick jump picker; swipe horizontally between months.
• FR-3.3: Tap a thumbnail → opens that day's Daily View with a shared-element (hero) transition.
• FR-3.4: Today is subtly outlined; future days are dimmed and non-interactive.
• FR-3.5: Long-press an empty past day → "Add photo for this day."
• FR-3.6: A Year overview (12 mini-month grids) is available via pinch-out or a segmented control.
• FR-3.7: Performance target: month swipe renders at 60 fps using pre-generated 200 px thumbnails.
3.4 Monthly & Yearly Contact Sheets
Monthly Contact Sheet
• FR-4.1: On the first launch after a month ends (and via a scheduled background job plus local notification, "Your October contact sheet is ready"), the app generates a collage of all photos in that month.
• FR-4.2: Months in progress show a live preview labeled "In progress"; the final sheet is generated automatically at month end and can be regenerated on demand if the user edits entries.
• FR-4.3: Visual style: contact-sheet aesthetic with thin gutters, small date numerals under each frame, a month/year header and an optional film-strip border. Frames keep their original aspect ratios (no destructive cropping).
• FR-4.4: Deep-linking: tapping any tile opens that entry's Daily View. Implementation: every tile is a hit region mapped to an entry_id; navigation uses the route /day/{yyyy-MM-dd}.
• FR-4.5: Back navigation from a Daily View returns to the same scroll/zoom position of the sheet.
• FR-4.6: Export/share the sheet as a high-res JPEG/PNG (location-free).
• FR-4.7: Missing days appear as blank frames (default) or are collapsed (user setting "Compact layout").
Yearly Collage
• FR-4.8: A year view showing all entries of the year, either as a 12-row monthly strip layout (one row per month) or a dense mosaic (user toggle).
• FR-4.9: Same deep-link behavior; pinch-to-zoom for dense mosaics with progressive image loading.
• FR-4.10: Auto-generated each January 1 for the prior year, and available live for the current year.
Edge cases: Months with 0 photos produce no sheet (the card is hidden). Months with 1–4 photos use a "gallery" layout rather than a sparse grid. Edited or replaced photos invalidate the cached sheet automatically (via a content hash).
3.5 Print-Ready Postcards
Flow
1. User opens Postcards → selects 12–15 photos from anywhere in the journal (multi-select grid with a counter, "12 of 12–15").
2. Chooses a format (see below) and style: Full-bleed photo, White border, Photo + caption (date/title), or Photo front + message area on the back.
3. Reviews each postcard in a swipeable preview with trim, bleed and safe-zone guides togglable.
4. Exports a print-ready PDF (single multi-page file, front and back pages in the order printers expect) or sends the order directly to a print partner (V1.5).

• Minimum 4.5:1 text contrast; 44 × 44 pt touch targets.
• Alt text defaults to the entry title; users may add a dedicated description for VoiceOver/TalkBack.

5.4 Storage Strategy
• Originals: copied into the app's private documents directory (/photos/orig/{id}.jpg|heic) so the journal survives gallery deletions.
• Derivatives (generated at import): thumb_200, medium_800, large_2048 as JPEG/WebP under /photos/derived/.
• Cloud sync (Plus): upload originals plus sidecar JSON to Supabase Storage using resumable uploads; sync metadata rows over Postgres. Conflict rule: last-write-wins by updated_at, but photo-replacement conflicts move the loser to trash rather than overwriting it.
• Backups: include the DB and /photos/orig in iCloud/Google Auto Backup, and provide a manual "Export journal (ZIP)" option.
6. Core Technical Implementations
6.1 Efficient EXIF Extraction on Mobile
Principles
1. Extract once, at import, and persist. Never parse EXIF during scrolling or rendering.
2. Read from the original file, not from a resized or recompressed copy; resizing usually strips EXIF.
3. Run off the UI thread (an isolate or native code).
Platform pitfalls (important)
• iOS: image_picker can return a re-encoded image with EXIF/GPS removed. Use photo_manager (PhotoKit PHAsset) and request the original data to retain metadata. Location is also available from PHAsset.location.
• Android 10+: location data is redacted unless the app declares and requests ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION; use MediaStore.setRequireOriginal(uri) to read the unredacted file. The system Photo Picker may also redact location, so request the permission explicitly and test on real devices.
• HEIC/HEIF: pure-Dart parsers have inconsistent support; prefer native readers (ImageIO / ExifInterface) and fall back to the Dart package for JPEG.
• Time zones: DateTimeOriginal has no zone; use OffsetTimeOriginal (EXIF 2.31) if present, otherwise assume the device's zone and set tz_assumed = true.

Reference pipeline (Dart sketch)
Future<PhotoMeta> importPhoto(AssetEntity asset) async {
  final file = await asset.originFile;                // original bytes, EXIF intact
  final stored = await copyToAppDir(file!);           // private copy
  final meta = await Isolate.run(() => parseExif(stored.path)); // off UI thread
  final derivs = await generateDerivatives(stored);   // thumb/medium/large
  final place  = meta.hasGps
      ? await reverseGeocode(meta.lat!, meta.lng!)     // cache result
      : null;
  return PhotoMeta(meta, derivs, place);
}
Normalization: convert GPS DMS rationals to decimal degrees; swap width/height according to orientation; store shutter speed as a rational (numerator/denominator) and aperture as a decimal so formatting can change without reparsing; keep the raw EXIF in a JSON blob column for future features.
Efficiency tips: for JPEG, read only the first 64–128 KB of the file (the APP1 segment) when using a Dart parser; batch imports (e.g., backfilling many days) with a concurrency of 2–3 isolates; rate-limit reverse geocoding and cache results by rounded coordinates (~3 decimals).
6.2 Generating Monthly/Yearly Collages Without Lag
Strategy: pre-compute the layout, pre-generate the pixels, cache the result.
1. Layout is pure math. Compute tile rectangles from photo aspect ratios in an isolate and store them as lightweight JSON ([{entryId, x, y, w, h}], normalized 0–1). Use a justified-rows algorithm: for each row, add photos until the row's total aspect ratio × target height reaches the container width, then scale the row to fit. For yearly mosaics use a squarified treemap or fixed-grid packing.
2. Interactive view ≠ exported image.
    ◦ On-screen: render with a CustomPainter (or a Stack of positioned Image widgets for ≤ 31 tiles) using the 200–800 px derivatives. For the year (≈ 365 tiles) use a single CustomPainter with drawImageRect and a tile cache, or InteractiveViewer with level-of-detail loading: show thumb_200 at zoom ≤ 1.5×, then switch visible tiles to medium_800 when zoomed in.
    ◦ Exported image: render once at high resolution (e.g., 3000 px wide) using dart:ui PictureRecorder + Canvas.drawImageRect, encode to JPEG/PNG, and save to /collages/{type}_{period}_{hash}.jpg.
3. Hit-testing for deep links. Keep the normalized rects from step 1; on tap, convert the tap position to normalized coordinates, find the containing rect, and call context.go('/day/$date'). This works identically for the on-screen and exported layouts.
4. Caching & invalidation. Compute content_hash = SHA-1(sorted [entryId + photo_updated_at + layout_version + style]). If the cache row has the same hash, reuse the file; otherwise regenerate in the background. Regeneration is triggered by entry add/replace/delete and at month end.
5. Memory discipline. Decode images at target size using ResizeImage / cacheWidth; never decode originals for collages; dispose ui.Image objects after drawing; limit concurrent decodes to ~4.
6. Month-end automation. Use workmanager (Android) and BGTaskScheduler (iOS) for a daily "collage check" task; on the first foreground launch of a new month also check and generate lazily (OS schedulers are best-effort). Send a local notification when the sheet is ready.
7. Performance budgets: monthly sheet generation < 1.5 s on a mid-range device; yearly mosaic first paint < 500 ms from a cached layout; 60 fps scrolling with no frame > 16 ms in a profile build.
6.3 Generating Print-Ready PDF Postcards
Goal: a PDF a commercial printer can accept without manual fixes.
A. Page geometry (PDF points; 72 pt = 1 in = 25.4 mm)
• Convert mm → points: pt = mm × 72 / 25.4.
• US 4×6 with bleed: 4.25 × 6.25 in = 306 × 450 pt.
• A6 with bleed: 111 × 154 mm ≈ 314.65 × 436.54 pt.
• Set the PDF MediaBox to the bleed size, and define TrimBox (inset by the bleed) and BleedBox (= media box). The Dart pdf package sets the page format to the bleed size; if a print partner requires an explicit TrimBox, post-process with a small server function (e.g., pdf-lib in an Edge Function) to inject the boxes.
• Margins/safe zone: place all text inside trim + 5 mm. Photos run to the bleed edge in full-bleed layouts.
B. Resolution (DPI)
• Target 300 DPI at final size: the bleed-size pixel dimensions listed in §3.5 (e.g., 1275 × 1875 px for US 4×6).
• Compute effective DPI per photo: effectiveDpi = croppedPixelWidth / placedWidthInInches. Show a badge: ≥ 250 green, 200–249 amber, < 200 red.
• Do not upscale low-res images to fake DPI; warn instead. Optional server-side AI upscaling can be a premium add-on later.
• Embed images as JPEG at quality 90–95. Pre-crop to the target rectangle in an isolate so the PDF stays manageable (target < 3 MB per page).
C. Color: RGB vs. CMYK Phones capture sRGB (or Display P3); printers print CMYK. Recommended approach:
1. MVP: export sRGB PDFs with an embedded sRGB ICC profile (or PDF/X-4, which allows RGB with an output intent). Many modern print partners accept RGB and convert in their RIP using tuned profiles, which usually beats a naive client-side conversion. Confirm this with each partner.
2. V1.5 (professional/DIY printing): a server-side conversion step using Ghostscript (-sColorConversionStrategy=CMYK -dProcessColorModel=/DeviceCMYK) or LittleCMS with a target profile such as FOGRA39 (EU) or U.S. Web Coated SWOP v2 / GRACoL (US). Ship it as an Edge Function or a small container (e.g., Cloud Run).
3. Convert Display P3 → sRGB before embedding to avoid color shifts.
4. Warn users that saturated blues and greens print less vividly than on-screen. For small text on CMYK backs, use single-channel black (100K) to avoid registration problems.
D. Bleed & crop marks
• Full-bleed layout: the photo fills the whole bleed-size page (cover-fit using the user's focal point).
• Bordered layout: draw the border color to the bleed edge (never leave white strips that rely on exact trim), then inset the photo.
• Crop marks (offset 3 mm outside the bleed) are only added under an Export for professional printer toggle, since most online printers do not want them.
E. Document structure
• One PDF containing all postcards: for each card, a front page then a back page (duplex order). Optionally offer an imposed sheet (e.g., 4 cards per A4/Letter with crop marks) for print shops.
• Embed font subsets (use SIL OFL fonts).
• Re-encoding strips photo EXIF, so no GPS is carried into the PDF.

G. Automated preflight checklist
• All images ≥ 200 effective DPI (warn) / ≥ 150 (block).
• Page size equals trim + 2 × bleed within ±0.1 mm.
• Text inside the safe zone.
• Fonts embedded; transparency flattened if the printer requires PDF/X-1a.
• File size within the partner's limit.

  name       TEXT NOT NULL,
  format     TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- 'US_4x6' | 'A6' | 'US_5x7'
  style      TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- full_bleed | border | caption
  status     TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'draft',  -- draft | exported | ordered
  pdf_path   TEXT,
  created_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
  updated_at INTEGER NOT NULL
);

CREATE TABLE postcard_items (
  id           TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  set_id       TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES postcard_sets(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  entry_id     TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES entries(id),
  position     INTEGER NOT NULL,             -- order in set
  crop_x REAL, crop_y REAL, crop_w REAL, crop_h REAL, -- normalized, non-destructive
  caption_text TEXT,
  back_message TEXT,
  UNIQUE(set_id, position)
);

-- Key/value settings (theme, reminder time, default format, etc.)
CREATE TABLE settings (key TEXT PRIMARY KEY, value TEXT);
Integrity notes
• entries.entry_date UNIQUE is the database-level guarantee of the one-photo-per-day rule; the UI rule is a courtesy on top. If soft-deleted rows should free up the date, use a partial unique index (WHERE deleted_at IS NULL) instead of a column-level UNIQUE.
• Replacing a photo: insert the new photos row, update entries.photo_id in one transaction, set trashed_at on the old row; a daily job purges after 30 days.
• Enforce 12–15 items per postcard set in the domain layer (validated at export, not by the DB, so drafts can be incomplete).
• Add SQLite FTS5 on entries(title, description) in V2 for search.
• The stable deep-link key is the date string YYYY-MM-DD (human-readable and unique), not the UUID.

• One-time "Lifetime" purchase (≈ $59–$79) for users averse to subscriptions.
• Gift cards for postcard packs.
• No advertising and no data sales. Privacy is a brand pillar and simplifies compliance (GDPR, India's DPDP Act).
8.4 Unit Economics Snapshot (illustrative)
• Cloud cost per Plus user: ~1–3 GB at typical object-storage rates plus egress, likely under $0.10/user/month.
• Store fees: 15% (small-business or year-two subscription rates) to 30%.
• Target blended ARPU (subscription + print) of $12–$20 per year among paying users.
• Physical goods are generally exempt from in-app purchase commissions, so route print orders through your own checkout, but verify current App Store and Google Play guidelines before launch.
9. Step-by-Step Implementation Roadmap
Team assumption: 2 Flutter engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM/QA, and part-time backend help. Timelines are indicative.
Phase 0 – Discovery & Foundations (Weeks 1–2)
• Finalize brand, design tokens and a clickable Figma prototype (Daily View, Calendar, Contact Sheet, Postcard flow).
• Run 8–10 user interviews and a landing-page waitlist test.
• Set up the repo, CI/CD (GitHub Actions + Fastlane or Codemagic), dev/stage/prod flavors, Sentry and linting.
• Spike: validate EXIF extraction on ~20 real photos/devices (iPhone HEIC, Pixel, Samsung, DSLR JPEG) including Android location permissions.
• Spike: place a sample print order with a partner to validate PDF specs end to end.
Phase 1 – MVP: The Daily Ritual (Weeks 3–8)
Scope
• Onboarding, photo permissions, light/dark theme.
• Add photo (gallery picker, camera capture); one-per-day enforcement, replace flow, backfill.
• Local Drift DB, file storage and derivative generation.
• EXIF extraction pipeline, metadata drawer and reverse geocoding.
• Daily View with title/description editing; swipe between days.
• Calendar month grid with thumbnails.
• Daily reminder notification; ZIP export of the journal.
Exit criteria: 50 beta users (TestFlight / Play internal) with ≥ 40% reaching 14 entries in 30 days; crash-free sessions > 99.5%.
Phase 2 – Contact Sheets & Collages (Weeks 9–13)
• Justified-row layout engine (isolate) and CustomPainter renderer.
• Monthly contact sheet with in-progress preview and month-end generation (workmanager / BGTaskScheduler + local notification).
• Deep-link navigation from tiles to days, with state restoration.
• Yearly collage (strip + mosaic modes) with progressive zoom.
• High-res, location-stripped export/share.
• Cache with content-hash invalidation; profiling on low-end Android.
Exit criteria: monthly sheet < 1.5 s on a mid-range device; 100% tap-through accuracy in QA; no jank in profile mode.
Phase 3 – Postcards & Public Launch (Weeks 14–19)
• Multi-select (12–15) with counter and validation.
• Crop/focal-point editor (non-destructive), layout styles, back-side template.
• PDF generator with bleed, safe zone, 300 DPI, effective-DPI badges and preflight checks.
• Preview with trim/bleed overlays; share/save PDF.
• Paywall plumbing (RevenueCat) and Plus-tier gating.
• Launch on App Store and Google Play; ASO, press, Product Hunt.
Exit criteria: 20 test PDFs accepted by two print vendors without manual correction; subscription purchase flow verified in sandbox and production.
Phase 4 – V1.5: Sync & Print Ordering (Weeks 20–28)
• Supabase auth (Sign in with Apple / Google / email), encrypted storage, resumable uploads.
• Multi-device sync with the conflict handling described in §3.1 and §5.4.
• In-app "Order postcards" checkout through the print API (address validation, tracking, webhooks, order history).
• Server-side CMYK / PDF-X conversion for professional export.
• Funnel analytics dashboards; A/B tests on paywall and pricing.
Phase 5 – V2: Depth & Delight (Months 7–12)
• Home-screen and lock-screen widgets ("today's frame", "on this day").
• On-this-day resurfacing; full-text search and a map view of entries.
• Annual hardcover Yearbook and poster products.
• Additional postcard formats (5×7, panoramic) and premium contact-sheet themes.
• Optional private sharing (read-only link to a month or year).
• Apple Watch / Wear OS quick capture; Shortcuts/Siri integration.
• Accessibility audit and localization (Hindi, Spanish, German, French, Japanese).
• Optional opt-in AI features: upscaling for low-res prints, caption suggestions.
